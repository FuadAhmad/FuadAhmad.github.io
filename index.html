Fuad Ahmad

Short Bio

I am a PhD student in Computer Science at New Mexico State University (NMSU), Las Cruces, NM, working as a Graduate Research Assistant. My research sits at the intersection of machine learning, data mining, and large language models. I develop class-wise discriminative feature selection methods for high-dimensional data with severe class imbalance, with applications to network intrusion detection, and I study how large language models can make technical information more usable for non-expert audiences.

My recent work includes nCMD: Benign-Anchored Feature Selection for Imbalanced Network Intrusion Detection (IEEE MILCOM 2026) and a review of transfer learning techniques for large language models (International Journal of Data Science and Analytics, Springer Nature, 2026). I serve on the program committee of IEEE ICTAI 2026 and review for IEEE ICMLA 2026, and IEEE Transactions on Artificial Intelligence.

Before returning to graduate study I spent over five years building mobile applications and games, shipping titles with more than a million cumulative downloads — experience that still shapes how I think about building systems that work in practice.
