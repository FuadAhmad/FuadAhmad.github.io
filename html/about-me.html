Fuad Ahmad

My Daily Life

Background

I am from Bangladesh. I grew up in a village where there was no electricity while I was in high school. My father, who could not finish his college due to the hardship of life, always expected and did everything to make sure I finish my engineering degree. I have two younger siblings studying in college and in a university, whom I need to support sometimes. I hope I will graduate soon then get a decent job and will help others.

Career Goal

I am pursuing a PhD in Computer Science at New Mexico State University, focusing on machine learning, data mining, and large language models. My goal is to become a research scientist working on trustworthy machine learning for security-critical and high-impact systems. I am drawn to complex, large-scale, real-world problems, and I enjoy work that pushes me outside my existing comfort and knowledge set.

What I Work On

Much of my research asks a simple question: which features in a dataset actually carry the signal, and how do we find them when the classes are wildly imbalanced? In network intrusion detection, benign traffic can outnumber attacks by orders of magnitude, and methods that look strong on paper generate false alarms that overwhelm the analysts who have to act on them. My recent work develops benign-anchored feature selection to address exactly this problem.

I also work with large language models — both in applying them, such as turning dense ecological drought data into summaries that decision-makers can actually use, and in studying them, including transfer learning techniques and low-latency safety guardrails for prompt filtering.

Why This Matters

Machine learning now sits behind decisions that affect people directly: what gets flagged as an intrusion, which scans warrant a closer look, what information reaches a community facing drought. That makes the unglamorous parts — missing values, imbalanced data, redundant features, false alarms — the parts that determine whether a system helps or quietly fails the people relying on it. I want my work to contribute to methods that hold up outside the benchmark.
